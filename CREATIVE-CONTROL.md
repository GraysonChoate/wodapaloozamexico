# WODAPALOOZA MEXICO CITY — CREATIVE CONTROL

**Canonical working context for Codex and Claude. Read before proposing, building, or
approving any new work.**

This document exists because the project has three equal jobs. It is not enough to
finish a technically strong scroll film if it remains only a film in a browser.

## The actual outcome

Build a silent, cinematic, source-honest scroll experience that moves like a short
film **and** feels like a distinctive Wodapalooza Mexico event website: a reader must
experience atmosphere, story, clear moments of orientation, and decisive ways to act.

The current build has a strong **film skeleton and motion grammar**. The next work must
not lose the two unfinished layers that make it feel authored and useful:

1. **Campaign-print world** — graphic, physical, editorial Mexico identity.
2. **Official event / conversion system** — verified facts, links, hierarchy, and
   recognizable places to pause and act.

These are not “later decoration.” They are part of the final product.

### Client-footage coverage rule

Every distinct, non-duplicate client video clip is valuable and must have an intentional
home in the finished experience. A clip may serve as a full beat, a panel in the Beat 5
city atlas, a crop inside type, a contained editorial plate, or a very short transition
detail. It may not be inserted as a meaningless flash merely to satisfy a count.

Before final sign-off, create a clip-coverage map: **source file → exact beat(s) → role →
visible range**. If a source is currently unused, find the most narratively honest place to
use it; do not discard strong client material simply because the first edit did not need it.

The full-film review follows this decision. It must use continuous normal-speed desktop
and mobile recordings, not only isolated screenshots or stress-test flings. It reviews
cadence, scroll distance, holds, transitions, copy arrival, reverse behavior, and the
entire four-act emotional arc.

### B. Campaign-print / material world — Codex leads, Claude integrates after review

The visual reference supplied by the operator establishes a missing layer: physical,
graphic Mexico campaign energy. It is the **syrup and cherry**, not a blanket filter.

We need a compact campaign-print material kit, informed by the highest-resolution supplied

- rough paper fields and fibres;
- true printed halftone;
- ink wear, brush/spray energy, and misregistration;
- hard-edged landmark fragments and architectural crops;
- sticker edges, adhesive contact, banner fabric, concrete/limestone grain;
- bold black sign geometry and official mark treatments.

The current source findings and asset boundary are recorded in
`reference/CAMPAIGN-MATERIAL-MAP.md`.
The candidate component set and its allowed uses are recorded in
`reference/CAMPAIGN-ASSET-EXTRACTION-PLAN.md`.

Use it sparingly as a set of editorial objects: a graphic surface arrives, holds
important copy or an action, and releases back into film. This creates actual *website
places*.

Likely homes, subject to full-film review:

- Beat 7 street/sticker world: source-real abrasion, sticker contact, or poster fragment.
- Beat 8: quiet continuity with real paper without adding synthetic paper on top.
- selected chapter thresholds: contained campaign fragment or print plate as orientation.
- final action index: links presented as a purposeful campaign/editorial object, not loose
  text over a video.

Never use this layer to grade footage, fake photographed light, add generic CSS magenta,
or blanket every beat in grain. Do not recreate campaign artwork from a low-resolution
screenshot. Original generated print material is encouraged when it is clearly page-owned,
contains no invented copy/logos/facts, and does not impersonate documentary photography.

Do **not** import SoCal’s orange UI, ordinary event-page layout, permanent navigation,
or California styling. The goal is a film with editorial action places, not a standard
festival portal.

## Framing hierarchy — the site must not default to fullscreen video

The finished experience is not a video player with scroll control. Fullscreen footage is
reserved for a dominant claim, a major world-expansion, a match cut, or a moment that needs
the whole frame. Other material should be allowed to exist as an object:

- a contained photograph or moving plate on black;
- a crop window that expands or collapses;
- a small field of related planes that registers into one image;
- a physical poster, sticker, paper, or action object;
- a held editorial surface where a reader can orient, read, or choose.

Every contained/multiple-frame move must explain a real relationship in the material. It may
not become a generic carousel or a series of arbitrary thumbnails. Daytime overhead and
foot-traffic material is a candidate for this kind of framed, spatial choreography—not an
excuse to substitute an unrelated night aerial strip. The supplied `Other B-Roll/` recordings
are approved working material for this refinement phase.

## Transition rule — every change of scene earns its frame

No scene may simply become the next scene because the scroll reached a boundary. Before any
different location, scale, or visual world arrives, the outgoing and incoming frames must share
an intentional relationship that a viewer can feel: a dissolve/release, an expansion from a
contained object, a collapse into a printed field, a spatial match, a purposeful occlusion, or
a clearly motivated editorial step. A hard cut is permitted only when the two frames already
form an explicit visual sentence; it is never a default transition. Judge the transition in
continuous, normal-speed scrolling, not from isolated stills. The Beat 0 black-plate → cockpit
handoff remains a flagged whole-film-review item, not a license to make unexamined cuts elsewhere.

### Closing HUD decision

The HUD belongs to the flight. Let `FLOOR` remain readable as Beat 13 finishes and the genuine
black hold begins, then release the HUD before the action-index hairline/paper arrives. The
printed action destination carries its own mark and must not compete with a second, floating
film mark or an altitude instrument that has completed its job.

## Non-negotiables

- English-led, Spanish where it lands hardest; words remain scarce.
- Source-native footage colour remains untouched. Applied interface accents stay
  green → blue with black, white, and neutral.
- Applied texture is encouraged when it clearly reads as **page-owned graphic material**—not
  fake light or a colour correction to photography. Author and generate a controlled print
  kit: paper tooth, ink/spray marks, halftone fields, dry-brush energy, registration shifts,
  and torn/printed-edge language.
- Generated print assets must never impersonate client photographs, fabricate a documentary
  city/athlete scene, carry invented event facts, or contain invented logos/text. They live
  around, between, and behind the footage as authored page design.
- The campaign-print layer may use its own source-inspired cyan, magenta, yellow, and black
  inside discrete graphic objects. This does not change the rule that the persistent UI system
  stays green → blue, black, white, and neutral, and it never authorizes colour-grading footage.
- No unlicensed external footage, fabricated scene, invented venue/facts, watermark retouching,
  or partly clipped watermarks.
- A watermark must be naturally out of frame or shown whole.
- Normalidad replaces Archivo when the Adobe kit is provided; typography is a structural
  layer, not a late afterthought.
- Do not apply a cool move merely because a reference used it. Every action requires a
  source-supported narrative reason.

## Mandatory decision check

Before any future direction, audit, or build instruction, state which track(s) it affects:

- film/motion;
- campaign-print/material world;
- official event/action system;
- typography/copy;
- whole-film pacing and QA.

If a proposal ignores one of the active tracks, it is incomplete unless it explicitly says
why that track is unaffected. Do not reduce the project to “finish the remaining effect.”

## Synchronization rule

`CREATIVE-CONTROL.md` and the active Claude handoff are one coordinated instruction set.
Whenever a project decision changes this document, Codex must immediately revise and resend
the active Claude instruction in the same turn. Claude must treat the newest handoff as
superseding earlier prompts where they differ. No agent may continue from a stale summary.
