# TRANSITION PASS — TWO PROOFS ONLY

**Tracks:** film/motion; campaign-print/material world; whole-film pacing and QA.

The final whole-film audit (`audit/whole-film/`, commit `3176477`) found the single largest
remaining defect: thirteen scene boundaries use the same 0.12–0.16 second black dip. The goal is
not to replace each with an effect. Build two source-supported transitions that establish a small,
repeatable vocabulary, then stop for review.

## 1. Beat 0 → Beat 1 — city plate becomes cockpit aperture

**Problem:** The Beat 0 framed Ángel plate and the cockpit windshield already share a meaningful
landscape geometry, but a generic black dip separates them. The intended match never occurs.

**Sentence:** *The city, considered as a printed object, becomes the view through which the
descent begins.*

**Opening quality bar — new, binding:** A symmetric image shrinking into a centered box over an
empty field is only a structural proof, never an automatic final transition. The plate must feel
*moved by a form* that belongs to the print/cockpit relationship. Before declaring this boundary
approved, capture three brief motion variants using the same material and timing:

- **Diagonal registration:** offset crop edges or print-registration bars travel diagonally,
  narrowing the city and revealing the cockpit inside their moving aperture.
- **Recessed plate:** the city retreats into the print field with a slight authored perspective
  and an asymmetric anchor; its released rectangle becomes the windshield aperture.
- **Windshield lock:** the city plate resolves directly into the cockpit's windshield geometry,
  with one edge arriving before the others rather than a uniform centre collapse.

Choose only the version that remains clear at normal scroll speed, preserves the city/cockpit
sentence, and creates purposeful negative space. Do not add ornament merely to make the move
busy. The current centered-collapse treatment may remain only if it wins this comparison in
continuous playback.

**Build:**

1. The final Beat 0 moving plate must hold its last geometry; do not let the page fade globally
   to black between sources.
2. Introduce the first cockpit frame **inside that exact plate/aperture**. A short internal
   replacement or contained dissolve is allowed; it must not look like two unrelated full-screen
   clips fading through black.
3. Only after the cockpit owns the aperture may the aperture expand/release into the existing
   full cockpit beat. The HUD arrives with the cockpit, not before.
4. Preserve the existing source timing and the Beat 0 print hold. Do not revive the rejected
   night-aerial prelude.

**Proof of success:** The boundary has zero generic black frames. Paused at the swap, both states
occupy the same aperture. Forward and reverse read as one relationship, not a jump. The chosen
motion must also pass the opening quality bar above; do not call a plain centered contraction a
cinematic solution by default.

## 2. Beat 6 → Beat 7 — banner claim registers into street action

**Problem:** The strongest thematic rhyme in the film—claiming the city with a physical banner,
then physically placing a mark in the street—is currently flattened by the generic dip.

**Sentence:** *The public claim is registered into the physical act.*

**Build:**

1. Before changing timings, inspect the final useful Beat 6 frame and the first useful Beat 7
   frame. The banner/claim must remain readable; do not force the late stairs if they cannot
   carry the relationship.
2. Use a temporary, page-owned **registration aperture**: two restrained black/neutral bars or
   crop edges close the outgoing claim into a narrow strip; inside the same strip, the Beat 7
   street/pole placement appears; the edges release to the existing Beat 7 frame.
3. This is print-registration / poster-to-sticker logic, not sponsor UI, a generic wipe, a
   magenta wash, or a carousel. Any cyan/magenta/yellow may live only on the existing campaign
   material edge; footage stays ungraded.
4. The release must finish before the active Beat 7 montage takes over. Preserve the footage,
   copy, source-native colour, and existing Beat 7 internal rhythm.

**Proof of success:** No generic black dip. The viewer can describe the transition as banner →
mark placement without being told. It reverses cleanly and is legible at desktop and mobile.

## Required delivery

For each transition: desktop/mobile forward and reverse recordings, a six-state contact sheet,
one boundary measurement showing no generic black run, no-JS/reduced-motion result, performance
result, and an exact list of files/lines changed. Do not modify any other boundary, copy, HUD,
navigation, action index, or texture surface.

After both are built, stop for Codex review. The next task is a single mid-film framed
interruption—not a broader transition sweep.
