## TYPEFACE

**Normalidad**, by CSTM Fonts — Ilya Ruderman & Yury Ostromentsky. Available on Adobe Fonts.

*Method: computed `font-family` on rendered text at wodapalooza.com, counted by frequency.*

| role | family | weights observed |
|---|---|---|
| display | `normalidad-wide` | 400, 600, 700, 800, 900 |
| body | `normalidad-text` | 600, 800 |
| legacy / secondary | `proxima-nova` | 400 |

**Why this matters more than a normal font note.** Normalidad is a **variable font with two
axes — width AND weight.** Compact through UltraExtended, Thin through Black. 30 static
styles, 12 variable files.

That means width is animatable. Type can physically *expand* as the reader scrolls — not a
fade, not a slide, an actual change in the letterforms' proportion. Most brands cannot do
this because their typeface has no width axis. Theirs does. **This is the single most
distinctive typographic move available on this project and it should carry a signature
moment.**

Fallback stack when Normalidad is unavailable:
`"normalidad-wide", "Archivo Expanded", "Helvetica Neue", Arial, sans-serif`

## PALETTE

### THE MEXICO CITY PALETTE — authoritative

*Method: these three hex values are written INSIDE `USOS_LOGO_WZA.pdf` as text, by the
designer (Sergio Garcia Mosqueda). They are the client's own stated values, not a sample.
An earlier pass sampled a colour-managed PNG render and got #85c18a / #5298c8 — close, but
desaturated. Always prefer the numbers written in the document.*

| hex | name | role |
|---|---|---|
| `#6CC180` | **brand green** | one of two approved solid grounds |
| `#35A7DF` | **brand blue** | the other approved solid ground |
| `#000000` | black | the primary ground |
| `#ffffff` | white | reversed mark, type |

**THE SIGNATURE IS A GRADIENT.** The badge outline runs green → blue, left to right.
Sampled across the stroke:

    far left    #73af84
    left-mid    #6aa998
    right-mid   #5fa2ae
    far right   #569cc2

That is a straight interpolation between the two brand grounds. **The gradient is the
identity** — it is what makes this Mexico City and not Miami, and it should be treated as a
material, not an effect: it can travel, it can light type, it can edge a frame.

### THE MIAMI PARENT PALETTE — for reference only, DO NOT LEAD WITH IT

*Measured from wodapalooza.com computed styles. This is the global brand, not the Mexico
edition.*

    #e6178b   magenta      Miami's signature
    #2da9e1   cyan
    #081f40   deep navy
    #020101   near-black
    #f5f5f5   off-white
    #33373d   charcoal

**Mexico runs COOL. Miami runs HOT.** This is the single most important brand distinction on
the project. An earlier draft of this document led with magenta — that was wrong, and it was
corrected once the client's own usage guide was read. Magenta may appear where WZA-global is
being referenced (the Miami footage, the LatAm Cup, the parent event), but the Mexico City
edition is green-to-blue on black.

## THE LONA ARTWORK IS NOT THE PALETTE

Measured off the banner in `STF00130`: cyan `#7EC3D6`, magenta `#DA5B97`.

These are the **LATAM Cup** artwork colours printed on one physical banner. They are recorded
here so nobody has to re-sample them, and to mark a boundary that is easy to state wrongly.

**The restriction is on APPLIED colour, not on colour that is in the picture.**

The palette — `#6CC180 → #35A7DF`, page 2 of the client's own usage guide — governs what the
*page paints*: CSS tokens, type, rules, gradients, washes, scrims, marks. Magenta is not in
that palette and must not be added to it.

It says nothing about what is allowed to be *in front of the camera*. The lona is magenta.
Miami footage is magenta. A CDMX taxi is pink. That colour is the material telling the truth
about itself, and it is never limited or counted. Grading it toward the Mexico palette would
be the real error: correcting the client's own record of their own event to match a swatch.

So: **never colour-correct source footage toward the palette**, and never promote a colour
sampled out of footage into a CSS token. The test is not whether magenta is on screen. It is
whether the page put it there.

Beat 6 shows these colours by showing the banner, ungraded, in its own afternoon light. A
`--lona-magenta` token at `:root` was written and then removed: an unused root token is still
a standing invitation to use it.

The same clip sampled in shade returns `#416A7C` — the lona reads muted in `LONA_0198` and
vivid in the stills. Same object, different light. Do not correct one to the other.
