## THE ONE IDEA

**Someone is claiming the city, monument by monument, and you never see their face.**

That is the campaign. A single figure in a black hoodie, hood up, back to camera, standing
in front of El Ángel de la Independencia, Monumento a la Revolución, Torre Latinoamericana,
Reforma. Arms raised in a double-bicep flex. The WZA Mexico City badge on their back is the
only identity in the frame.

You never see who it is **because it could be you.** That is not a stylistic choice, it is
the argument the whole event is making, and their own copy says it out loud:

> demostrar que **pertenecen** — prove that you belong
> **es momento de ganarte tu lugar** — it is time to earn your place

The figure has not earned it yet. They are staking the claim. The site should put the
visitor inside that hood.

## WHAT THE GUERRILLA CAMPAIGN ACTUALLY IS

Not advertising. **Occupation.**

18 photographs, all 4000×6000 or larger, shot around Mexico City's most symbolic
architecture. The same anonymous figure. And in several frames, that figure is physically
**hanging a banner** on the stone wall of Monumento a la Revolución — not posing beside it,
installing it.

The banner reads:

> **SUCEDERÁ EN CDMX**
> *It will happen in CDMX*

Future tense. Not an invitation — a statement of inevitability. Above it, the LATAM CUP
shield; below it, the WZA Mexico City badge.

Banner construction, worth stealing wholesale for the site:
- near-black textured ground
- **magenta halftone dot field** bleeding in from the edge
- headline in white on a **torn mint/cyan brush stroke**
- everything slightly weathered, creased, pasted

That is a street-poster aesthetic — halftone, torn edges, brush, paste. It belongs to the
city, not to a design system. **The site should feel pasted onto Mexico City rather than
floating above it.**

## WHAT THE FILM ACTUALLY IS

`WZA_MX_LOGO.mp4` — 58 seconds, 1920×1080, 23.976fps, with audio. The filename badly
undersells it: this is the full announcement film, and it is already a storyboard.

Its beat order:

    WELCOME TO MEXICO CITY        aerial, the city running to the mountains
    night drive                   POV from inside a car, CDMX plates
    the badge lands               WODAPALOOZA COMES TO MEXICO!
    straight-down aerials         the endless grid
    MIAMI FOOTAGE                 the stage, the crowd — proof of what this is
    SAVE THE DATE                 faces in a crowd, screaming
    FROM DECEMBER 4th TO 6th, 2026
    an athlete mid-lift           sponsor boards, full arena
    CELEBRATE FITNESS. COMMUNITY. LIFE.
    IN A CITY THAT HAS IT ALL.
    the Zócalo, the Ángel         monuments, dusk into night
    the badge locks onto Monumento a la Revolución

**It ends on a monument, not a stadium.** The film's own argument is that the city is the
venue — which is also how we sidestep the fact that no venue has been announced.

Note the structure is a **descent**: aerial → grid → street → inside a car → the floor. An
earlier draft of this brief called the spine "ascent" (sea level to altitude). The material
disagrees. **You do not climb to Mexico City. You arrive, and it swallows you.**

## THE TWO-PALETTE SYSTEM

This is the most useful structural discovery in the whole package.

| | palette | belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| **Mexico City** | green `#85c18a` → blue `#5298c8` gradient, on black | this edition |
| **WZA global / LatAm** | magenta `#e6178b`, cyan `#2da9e1` | the parent brand |

**Mexico runs cool. Miami runs hot.** And the guerrilla banner shows exactly how they meet:
magenta halftone as the incoming energy, mint-cyan brush as the local identity, both on
black.

That gives the site a real colour grammar rather than a swatch list:

- **Green→blue gradient** = Mexico City, the present, the thing being built
- **Magenta** = the global standard arriving, Miami, the LatAm pathway
- **Black** = the ground both sit on

When the site talks about Miami's legacy it can run hot. When it talks about December in
Mexico City it runs cool. The gradient is not decoration — **it is how the viewer knows
which story they are in.**

## THE PHOTOGRAPHIC LANGUAGE

Consistent across all 18 frames, and it should be matched by every treatment on the site:

- **Natural light only.** No flash, no artificial fill.
- **Filmic and slightly warm**, with soft highlight rolloff. Not clinical, not HDR.
- **Shallow depth of field** — the figure sharp, the monument softening behind.
- **Low angle**, looking up at both figure and architecture. Everything is monumental.
- **Wide skies.** Big pale clouds, hazy high-altitude blue.
- **Muted overall.** The black hoodie and the stone dominate; colour is scarce, which is
  exactly why the brand gradient reads so loudly when it appears.

**Do not grade this footage toward Miami.** The high-altitude haze and cool cast are the
differentiator, not a defect to correct.

## THE MONUMENTS

Recurring, deliberate, and they should become the site's spine:

- **El Ángel de la Independencia** — the golden victory column on Reforma
- **Monumento a la Revolución** — the enormous stone arch; where the banner gets hung
- **Torre Latinoamericana** — the tower on the skyline
- **The Zócalo** — the great square, seen empty at dusk and packed at night
- A red colonial building with palms — likely on or near Reforma

Each is a place a Mexican recognises instantly. Together they say: *this is not happening in
a convention centre on the edge of town. This is happening in the middle of the capital.*

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE SITE

1. **Put the visitor inside the hood.** Back-to-camera framing, first-person descent. The
   viewer is the anonymous figure, not a spectator watching them.
2. **Arrive, do not climb.** Aerial → grid → street → floor. Descent is the scroll spine.
3. **Claim monuments one at a time.** Each major section lands at a real Mexico City
   landmark, the way the campaign does.
4. **Use the gradient as a wayfinder.** Cool for Mexico/December, hot for Miami/global.
   The reader should feel the temperature change without being told.
5. **Paste it onto the city.** Halftone, torn brush edges, creased banner texture, weathered
   paste-up — the campaign's own materials, not generic grain.
6. **Future tense.** *Sucederá.* The whole site is written before the event, about something
   inevitable. That is a rarer and stronger register than "buy tickets".

## THE FOOTAGE — WHAT WE ACTUALLY HAVE

### One landscape film

`WZA_MX_LOGO.mp4` — 58s, **1920×1080**, 23.976fps, AAC audio, 14.9 Mbps. The announcement
film. Landscape. This is the only horizontal asset.

### Thirteen vertical clips

Every guerrilla clip is **1080×1920 — VERTICAL.** 30fps, h264, 24-bit PCM audio.

    lona 1/            3 clips   the banner unfurling down the monument wall
    ONLINE QUALIFIER/  1 clip    15.2s  the newspaper takeover
    Stickers 2/        1 clip    15.7s  stickers applied around the city
    Sudadera/          8 clips   the hooded figure at the monuments

**This is a hard structural constraint and it must shape the design, not be fought.** A
vertical clip letterboxed into a horizontal hero is the amateur move. Options that work:
vertical panels running side by side, a vertical filmstrip that scrolls horizontally as the
page scrolls down, split-screen columns, or full-bleed vertical on mobile where it is
native. The material was shot for a phone; a site that treats vertical as first-class will
look intentional rather than compromised.

### What is in the clips

**LONA** — the banner is *unfurled* down the black stone wall of Monumento a la Revolución.
It drops and hangs. That is a reveal with a real physical mechanic, and it maps directly
onto a scroll-driven mask or clip-path.

**ONLINE QUALIFIER** — the hooded figure stands in the street holding up a **real
newspaper** with the message written across it:

> EL ONLINE QUALIFIER
> **ES DEL 18 AL 26 DE AGOSTO**

The newspaper hides their face. The anonymity is maintained even here, and the message is
literally written over the news of the day. This is the post that appears on their
Instagram grid.

**STICKERS** — the WZA Mexico City badge stuck onto the city's real surfaces: a teal
lamppost, a **yellow** wall, a **purple** wall beside axolotl street art.

That last one is the quiet insight of the whole package. **The mark is designed to be a
sticker.** It is monoline black-and-white precisely so it survives being applied to a
surface that already has its own colour. Mexico City's actual palette — yellow walls,
purple murals, teal posts — becomes the ground, and the badge reads on all of it.

**That is the answer to the art-direction question.** We do not need to invent a Mexico City
palette to sit beside the brand. The campaign already demonstrated it: the brand is
black-and-white and it goes ON TOP of whatever colour the city already is.
