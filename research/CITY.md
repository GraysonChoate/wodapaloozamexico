## THE TRAP

The obvious move is *fiesta* Mexico — papel picado, marigold, sombrero palette, sugar
skulls. **It is the wrong city and the wrong event.** That vocabulary belongs to Oaxaca in
November, not to a functional-fitness championship in the largest metropolis in North
America.

Mexico City is **volcanic, brutalist, and high.** Grey basalt, enormous flat colour planes,
hard sun, thin air. That is the register. Anything decorative reads as a foreigner's idea
of Mexico, which is exactly what an event importing "el estándar de Miami" cannot afford to
look like.

---

## ALTITUDE IS THE STORY

**2,240 m / 7,350 ft.** Miami is at sea level.

This is not a marketing angle, it is a physical fact that every athlete on that floor will
feel in their lungs. It is the one thing this edition owns that Miami and SoCal can never
have.

It is also visually real. High-altitude light is different: harder shadows, longer golden
hour, a particular haze sitting in the Valley of Mexico. **Footage shot in CDMX should not
be graded to look like Miami footage.** The difference is the point.

Everything in this story moves upward — sea level to altitude, qualifier to competition
floor, 145 athletes to 2,000, belonging earned rather than given. **Ascent is the emotional
spine, and it happens to be the natural shape of a scroll.**

## FOUR MATERIALS

Every texture on the site should be traceable to one of these. No generic noise, no stock
grunge.

**1 · Volcanic stone (*piedra volcánica*)**
The black-grey basalt the city is built on and out of — walls, floors, the Ciudad
Universitaria campus. Porous, matte, absorbs light. This is the ground of the site. It sits
under a black-and-white badge perfectly.

**2 · Raw concrete**
Brutalist Mexico City. Flat, monumental, unapologetic. Provides the large calm surfaces
that let one loud colour do its work.

**3 · Thin high sky**
The cyan. At altitude the sky is a harder, cleaner blue than at sea level. `#2da9e1` is
already almost exactly this.

**4 · Chalk and sweat**
The sport's own material. Magnesium dust in hard light, the texture on a barbell knurl,
rubber, tape. This is where grain and particulate come from — **their material, not a
filter.**

## THE BARRAGÁN BRIDGE

Luis Barragán is Mexico City's colour language: vast flat planes of rosa mexicano, ochre
and deep pink meeting raw concrete under direct sun, with water and shadow doing the rest.

**Barragán's pink and Wodapalooza's magenta are cousins.** `#e6178b` reads as a modern,
electric descendant of *rosa mexicano*.

This is the whole art-direction thesis and it is not a stretch: **their existing brand
palette already IS Mexico City if you frame it correctly.** Nothing needs inventing. The
magenta stops being "event pink" and becomes a colour with 80 years of local architectural
authority behind it.

Reference points, all real and all local:
- Casa Luis Barragán / Cuadra San Cristóbal — pink planes, water, shadow
- Ciudad Universitaria (UNAM) — volcanic rock, muralism, monumental scale
- Museo Anahuacalli — Diego Rivera's black volcanic-stone building

## HOW THE TWO PALETTES MEET

    GROUND      volcanic black       #020101 / #05090a     the badge's own black
    SURFACE     raw concrete grey    #33373d / #69727d
    LIGHT       off-white            #f5f5f5 / #fefefe     the badge's own white
    HEAT        magenta              #e6178b               Barragán rosa, electrified
    AIR         cyan                 #2da9e1               thin high-altitude sky
    DEPTH       deep navy            #081f40               the valley at dusk

Nothing added, nothing borrowed. Six values, all of them either measured from the brand or
justified by the city.

## RULES

1. **No fiesta iconography.** No papel picado, no marigolds, no calaveras, no sombreros.
2. **Magenta is light, not paint.** It belongs to type, edges, rules and glow. Never a
   large flat field.
3. **Grain comes from chalk**, not from a noise filter. The particulate is the sport's.
4. **Do not correct the altitude light** out of the footage. It is the differentiator.
5. **Spanish leads.** Their own copy is Spanish-first. English, if present, is secondary.
6. **The city is the venue** until a venue is announced. This reads stronger than a hall
   name anyway, and it is honest — no venue is public.
